Real Client Data, Real Mess

Data Cleaning Challenges

Duplicate Product Names

Two nearly-identical product listings were being treated as separate SKUs due to a typo, splitting their sales and quietly distorting the refund rate calculated per product.

Arylic/Glitter→Acrylic&Glitter

Non-Product Transaction Lines

Over half the raw rows had no product name at all — not missing data, but legitimate shipping charge and refund reversal line items baked into the same order export. Filtered from product-level charts, kept in order-level totals.

Non-Product Revenue Line

A recurring "livestream deposit" line item — variable-amount pre-payments tied to the store's live-selling format — was inflating product-level revenue and unit counts. Identified and excluded at the source.

Overstated Units Sold

The original units-sold formula summed every line item regardless of refund status, overcounting units from fully refunded orders while correctly keeping partial refunds (where the item was still delivered).

343 units→297 units

What the Data Revealed

Key Insights

One Product Drives Over Half of Revenue

The top-selling item alone accounts for roughly half of total net sales, flagging a concentration risk worth monitoring for supply and demand shifts.

Refund Rate ≠ Refund Cost

The product with the highest refund rate (%) accounted for the smallest dollar loss, while the best-selling product — with a low refund rate — represented the largest absolute refund value due to sheer order volume.

Sharp, Unexplained January Spike

Revenue jumped roughly 3x above the surrounding months in January 2026. Cross-checked against the discount data to rule out a promo-driven cause, flagging it as an organic spike worth investigating further.

Corrected a Silent Metric Bug

An order status chart was found to be counting line-item rows instead of distinct orders, overstating totals by over 2x. Rebuilt using distinct order counts to match the rest of the dashboard.

Drill-Through for Order-Level Audit

Added a drill-through detail page so any product's underlying transactions — order ID, status, quantity, and refund value — can be pulled up directly from the summary chart.

98% Domestic Market

Nearly all orders (295 of 300) shipped within the US, with the rest split across the UK and Canada — confirming the business is currently a domestic-first operation.

How It Was Built

Analysis Process

1

Data Audit

Reviewed raw order export from the client's store, spotting typos, blank rows, and non-product line items before any chart was built.

2

Data Cleaning

Standardized duplicate product names, excluded non-product transaction lines, and fixed a units-sold formula to correctly handle refunds.

3

DAX Measures

Built measures for refund rate, refund dollar value, distinct order counts, and average order value, guarding against blank-value chart bugs.

4

Dashboard Build

Designed KPI cards and charts covering revenue trend, order status, and refund analysis from three angles: rate, dollar value, and by product.

5

Drill-Through Detail

Added a second page with a filterable order-level table, letting the client audit any product's transactions directly from the summary view.
